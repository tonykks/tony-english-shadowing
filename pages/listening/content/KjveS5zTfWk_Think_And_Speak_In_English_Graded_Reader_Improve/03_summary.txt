This two-step process acts as a mental bottleneck, making speech slow, hesitant, and prone to error. Using the story of a student named Marco, the speaker illustrates how this habit hinders fluency and creates unnecessary mental strain.

[Part 2]
EN: To overcome this, learners should stop viewing translation as a necessary step and instead look at how babies acquire language naturally through direct experience. Language is about connection and context rather than knowing every single word or maintaining 100% accuracy. By focusing on meaning rather than literal word-for-word translation, learners can bypass the slow intermediary steps of the two-step brain.

[Part 3]
EN: Building a 'one-step brain' requires intentional, daily practice to train the mind like a muscle. Effective techniques include starting with simple objects, talking to oneself, narrating the day, using circumlocution to describe unknown words instead of using a dictionary, and consuming English media without native-language subtitles. These habits help build direct neural connections between thoughts and English expressions.
KR: 일단계 뇌'를 구축하려면 근육처럼 마음을 단련하기 위한 의도적이고 매일의 연습이 필요합니다. 효과적인 기술로는 간단한 사물부터 시작하기, 혼잣말하기, 하루 일과 설명하기, 사전을 사용하는 대신 우회적인 표현으로 모르는 단어 설명하기, 그리고 모국어 자막 없이 영어 미디어 시청하기 등이 있습니다. 이러한 습관은 생각과 영어 표현 사이에 직접적인 신경 연결을 구축하는 데 도움이 됩니다.

[Part 4]
EN: Mistakes are an unavoidable and healthy part of the learning process, and fearing them only leads to silence, which is the true enemy of progress. The case of Marco shows that with consistent practice, students can overcome the initial discomfort of not translating. Eventually, this progress can lead to milestones like dreaming in the target language, signaling that the brain has begun to process English directly.

[Part 5]
EN: Ultimately, the objective of language learning is fluency rather than perfection. Even native speakers make errors, hesitate, and forget words, so learners should prioritize expressing their ideas over being flawless. By practicing consistently and staying patient, learners can move away from the slow process of translation and achieve a more natural, efficient way of thinking in English.
