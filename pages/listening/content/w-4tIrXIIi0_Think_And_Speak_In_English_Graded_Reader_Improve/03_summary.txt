[Part 2]
EN: The speaker uses a math analogy to explain why translation causes mental exhaustion. Because the brain's working memory can only process about four to seven pieces of information at once, constant translation consumes all available mental resources, leaving no room for natural conversation or fluidity.
KR: 화자는 왜 번역이 정신적 피로를 유발하는지 설명하기 위해 수학적 비유를 사용합니다. 뇌의 작업 기억은 한 번에 약 4개에서 7개의 정보만 처리할 수 있기 때문에, 끊임없는 번역은 가용할 수 있는 모든 정신적 자원을 소모하여 자연스러운 대화나 유창함을 위한 여유를 남기지 않습니다.

[Part 3]
EN: To overcome this hurdle, the speaker suggests bypassing the native language entirely and forming direct connections between thoughts and English. Using the example of how infants learn, the speaker emphasizes that language should be associated with actions, images, and feelings rather than direct translation, which makes the process more intuitive.

[Part 4]
EN: The speaker provides actionable steps to cultivate this new habit, such as labeling objects, describing one's day in short sentences, and having internal conversations in English. Additionally, changing device settings to English and writing simple daily diary entries help train the brain to transition to English without the need for an intermediary language.

[Part 5]
EN: Reflecting on Marco's eventual success, the speaker highlights that this transition is a skill developed through consistent, deliberate, and incremental practice rather than overnight mastery. By focusing on speed and natural expression over perfection, learners can eventually internalize the language, as demonstrated when Marco finally answered the 'weekend' question with natural ease.
